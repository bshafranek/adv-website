# Arkansas Trip

A 3-day round trip out of Edmond, OK into the Ozark and Ouachita Mountains of
Arkansas.

## Day 1 — Wednesday, August 26, 2026

**416.3 mi, ~7.9 hrs riding time, 8:11 AM – 7:42 PM**

Left Edmond heading east on I-40 through Shawnee, Henryetta, Checotah, and
Sallisaw, crossing into Arkansas near Fort Smith. Stopped and toured **Fort
Smith** before continuing on.

> Fort Smith was established in 1817 as a frontier military post on the Arkansas
> River, right at the edge of what was then Indian Territory. It's best known
> for its federal court under Judge Isaac Parker — the "Hanging Judge" — who
> presided from 1875 to 1896 over a jurisdiction covering 74,000 square miles of
> lawless Indian Territory, backed by a large force of deputy U.S. marshals sent
> out to track down outlaws (the same frontier that inspired *True Grit*). The
> old fort grounds, courthouse, and gallows are preserved today as the Fort Smith
> National Historic Site.

From Fort Smith the route turned north into the Ozark National Forest and onto
the **Pig Trail** (Arkansas Highway 23), climbing up toward Jasper and the
Ponca/Boxley area — Buffalo National River country, near Hawksbill Crag/Whitaker
Point, one of the most photographed overlooks in the state. The day ended in
**Ozark, AR**.

## Day 2 — Thursday, August 27, 2026

**332.0 mi, ~7.1 hrs riding time, 8:00 AM – 7:06 PM**

Back on the Pig Trail for more of its tight switchbacks and twisting,
canopy-covered curves through the Ozark National Forest — the kind of road that's
built its reputation as one of the best motorcycle roads in the state. Stopped
for breakfast at the **Oark Cafe** along the way.

> The Oark Cafe, in the tiny community of Oark (population measured in dozens),
> started life as a general store back in the 1890s. It's become a classic
> pilgrimage stop for riders working the Pig Trail and the surrounding Ozark
> byways — known for hearty country breakfasts and a genuinely old-school,
> ride-up-and-sit-on-the-porch kind of atmosphere.

From there the route pushed south toward Mena, AR, stopping at **Queen
Wilhelmina State Park** atop Rich Mountain.

> Queen Wilhelmina State Park sits at about 2,681 ft, the second-highest point
> in Arkansas, along the Talimena Scenic Byway that runs the ridge of the
> Ouachita Mountains along the Arkansas–Oklahoma line. Known as the "Castle in
> the Sky," the park takes its name from a lodge built there in the 1890s by
> Dutch railroad investors who hoped to build a resort and named it for the
> Netherlands' queen.

The route then headed back north and west, ending the day in **Muskogee, OK**.

## Day 3 — Friday, August 28, 2026

**134.1 mi, ~2.5 hrs riding time, 8:05 AM – 11:01 AM**

A short final leg: started in Muskogee and rode historic **Route 66** west back
home to Edmond.
